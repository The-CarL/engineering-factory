# Bounded project work

Agree concrete consequential scope for the requested app or change, then execute routine authorized work without repeated approval. Select operations from the workload and its dependencies: repository work, implementation, checks/CI, hosting, operations or recovery as needed. Reuse approved identities, targets, credential bindings and tooling. Programmatic `--confirm` arguments check targets.

The agent prepares scope from the product request and known private configuration, resolving routine choices and reusing existing exact authorization. A plan/profile/template alone grants no authority. Provider permissions and platform controls enforce actual access; names, tags and instructions do not. See [project guidance](../docs/new-project.md).

## Prepare applicable scope once

Keep actual values in a private project/run record. Include the fields relevant to the proposed work:

- Product outcome, workload/acceptance criteria, source/runtime revision, stable project/run identity and recovery/cleanup ownership.
- Exact repository/visibility, independent path/origin, allowed branches and repository/CI operations. Private is the default; other visibility needs explicit scope. No force push, deletion or unrelated repository access by implication.
- Selected provider targets and operations, including any new-resource/initial-release scope. Bind returned IDs only after exact name/ownership readback; matching names do not authorize adopting existing assets.
- Applicable protection, public exposure, domains/aliases, network recipients and test/operational access. No wildcard egress, token recipient expansion, protection bypass or paid upgrade by implication.
- Exact account/role/region/resources/actions/IaC profile where needed. Review policy changes before granting/applying them; installed credentials and identity reads do not establish write capabilities.
- Incremental budget, resource/build/request/polling/time limits and retention, including child/default resources. Estimates and counters are not hard provider spending limits; stop before exceeding scope.
- Any other-chat coordination: exact recipient title/ID/host, purpose and resume authority from the human. Otherwise work remains in the current task.

## First-deployment scope

Initial scope may authorize an app's first release with exact source/configuration, targets, exposure/protection, costs and recovery/rollback criteria. Verify the selected provider's actual classification and defaults before mutation. This grants no general production promotion or existing/core changes. Apply the relevant [provider contract](../docs/provider-workflows.md), including the [Vercel initial-release contract](../docs/provider-workflows.md#new-vercel-project-and-initial-release) when Vercel is selected.

## Execute and recover

Perform authorized implementation, repository, CI, hosting and verification work in the order appropriate to the app. Verify identities/targets before provider actions; record intent and reconcile exact outcomes/provenance. Continue independent authorized work when another capability is blocked.

A known terminal failure may receive a bounded corrected attempt when included in scope; preserve history and use a new operation record. Unknown outcomes require read-only reconciliation before another mutation. Resume exact verified resources; never clear a journal or change a name to duplicate or adopt them. Preserve source/private recovery artifacts durably and verify restoration when continuation depends on them.

Ask only for an actual scope/limit/identity/target/core-exposure/production/credential/IAM/protection change, unresolved consequential ambiguity or required action-time approval. Prepare the concrete target/diff and evidence first. A tool rejection blocks that action; explain its stated reason and continue unaffected work without using another route.
